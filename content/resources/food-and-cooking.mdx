Food in Valheim isn't optional flavor — it's how you raise your max health and stamina above their base values, and going into a boss fight on one or two active foods leaves real HP on the table. This covers how the system works and what to actually cook at each stage.

## How food works

You can have **up to three different food items active at once**, each contributing independently to your max HP, max stamina, and how fast both regenerate. You can't stack a second helping of an already-active food into a fourth slot — pick three different items to fill all three.

When a food's duration runs low, the bonus doesn't cut off all at once — it tapers down smoothly over the final stretch, and if your current HP is above the new, shrinking max, it drops with it. The practical upshot: top up a food before its timer runs out rather than after it expires, so your pool doesn't dip mid-fight.

## Cooking station progression

- **Campfire:** the base heat source. Every other cooking structure below needs an active Campfire (or the upgraded Hearth) placed underneath it to function.
- **Cooking Station:** available from the start — just 2 Wood and a nearby Workbench, no boss gate. Hangs raw meat and fish over the fire until cooked. An upgraded Iron Cooking Station appears later for higher-tier meats like Serpent and Lox.
- **Cauldron:** unlocks once you've smelted Tin at a working Forge, tying it to early Black Forest progression. Used for multi-ingredient dishes — stews, soups, pies.
- **Fermenter:** built from Fine Wood, Bronze, and Resin — the same Black Forest/early-Bronze window as the Cauldron. Turns mead bases into finished, drinkable meads over about two in-game days per batch. Needs a roof to function properly.
- **Mead Ketill:** a Forge-crafted station that makes mead bases (a role the Cauldron used to handle alone), feeding into the Fermenter.
- **Butcher Knife:** a Forge-crafted tool, not a station — the dedicated way to harvest meat and materials from tamed animals.
- **Windmill:** unlocks after defeating [Moder](/bosses/moder), built within an Artisan Table's radius. Converts Barley into Barley Flour, the key ingredient behind Bread, Fish Wraps, Blood Pudding, and Lox Meat Pie.

## What to cook, by era

**Meadows:** raw and cooked meat (deer, boar, neck tail), raspberries, blueberries, mushrooms, honey, and simple dishes like Carrot Soup. Short durations, but enough to get moving.

**Black Forest era:** once the Cauldron and Tin are available, Deer Stew, Sausages (meat + Thistle), and Queen's Jam (a berry-based Cauldron recipe) open up.

**Swamp era:** Turnip Stew and Black Soup, plus ingredients like Bloodbags that later feed into Blood Pudding.

**Mountain:** Wolf-meat dishes — Cooked Wolf Meat, Wolf Jerky, Wolf Skewer — suited to cold-biome survival.

**Plains (the "endgame" staple tier):** Serpent Stew, Lox Meat Pie, Bread and Fish Wraps (both need the Windmill), and Blood Pudding. These are the dishes most guides treat as the real high-value loadout once you're geared for Plains and beyond.

**Mistlands:** Eitr-boosting foods for magic builds — Yggdrasil Porridge, Seeker Aspic, Misthare Supreme, Fish 'n' Bread — which add an Eitr pool alongside the usual HP/stamina.

## Burning

Raw meat left on a Cooking Station past its cooked point doesn't stay edible forever — leave it too long and it burns into Coal instead. Cook times run roughly 20-60 seconds depending on the meat (Lox meat takes the longest), with burning following not far behind if you walk away. There's no rescuing burnt food back into meat, but the resulting Coal isn't wasted — it's still useful fuel. This burning risk is specific to raw Cooking Station meat; Cauldron stews and Fermenter meads don't have an equivalent failure state.

## Practical loadout advice

The standard trio is one HP-heavy food, one stamina-heavy food, and one hybrid — adjusted toward more HP for melee tanking or more stamina for a bow/mobility build. Fill all three slots with different foods before a boss fight or dangerous exploration; going in on one or two active foods is consistently flagged across guides as leaving damage capacity on the table. Carry backup cooked food on long expeditions too — running dry away from a cooking station means fighting at reduced max HP and stamina in hostile territory.
