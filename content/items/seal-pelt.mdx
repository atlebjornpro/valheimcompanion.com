## What it's used for


- Hunt seals along the [Deep North](/world/regions/deep-north) coast.
- Combine the pelt with Embers to make an [Ember Charge](/items/ember-charge).
- Use the charge on petrified Gammeltroll remains for [Petrified Tissue](/items/petrified-tissue).

## Coast farming and material priorities

Adult seals on the Deep North coast drop two to three Seal Pelts. Hunt from a position that lets you retrieve the body without losing it under ice or in deep water, and collect [Seal Blubber](/items/seal-blubber) during the same trip.

The urgent use is the [Ember Charge](/items/ember-charge): **two pelts plus one Ember make ten charges** at any workbench. That stack opens the Gammeltroll-to-[Bloodgold](/items/bloodgold) chain, so reserve the first pelts before decorating or crafting optional gear.

Pelts also appear in the Cape of the Caller, Vanguard hood and trousers casts, Moose Saddle, and Seal Rug. Those recipes compete with the boss-route supply. Keep a labeled chest beside the Eternal Pyre or portal hub and move pelts there before visiting the Black Forge. If the charge recipe is absent, obtain an Ember from the Eternal Pyre; the pelt alone does not reveal the complete recipe.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many pelts does an Ember Charge recipe need?

Two Seal Pelts and one Ember make ten charges at a workbench.

### Where do Seal Pelts come from?

Adult seals on the Deep North coast drop two to three pelts.

## Common mistakes

- Spending all pelts on armor before crafting the charges needed for Bloodgold.
- Bringing Embers and pelts to the wrong station; the recipe works at a workbench.
- Hunting on thin coastal edges where drops become hard to retrieve.

Reserve pelts in pairs because each recipe consumes two. One pair produces ten charges, which is usually enough to begin a Gammeltroll route. Keep any remaining pelts for the saddle and gear only after the three-key Bloodgold budget is secure.

Seals share the coast with difficult terrain, so build the retrieval plan into the attack. Fight toward land, watch where the body falls, and collect blubber and pelt before another target. A marked shoreline portal lets repeated short hunts supply charges without risking the full stack on open-water travel.
